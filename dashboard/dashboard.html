1. Capture

Label a stock in one click, right where you research

Open a stock page on a supported finance site and a small card appears in the corner of the page. Nothing to start, nothing to copy: the view is logged for you.

  • Ticker, time, price and source are recorded automatically on Yahoo Finance, Google Finance, MarketWatch, CNBC, Bloomberg, StockAnalysis, MarketBeat, Finviz, TradingView, Seeking Alpha, Nasdaq, Stocktwits, WSJ and Perplexity. Add any other site as a custom portal in Settings.
  • Say what you expect with one click: Positive, Neutral or Negative.
  • Optionally mark that you took a position, set a target price, choose a time horizon (1 week to 1 year) and write the one-line reason while it is fresh.
  • Reading behaviour is captured quietly in the background, with no extra clicks.

2. Measure

My Stocks: every pick, scored since you first looked

The watchlist ranks everything you have researched and shows what each stock did after your first view. Click a name to see it on the chart.

  • Summary bar with your totals: tickers tracked, daily events, intraday views, label hit rate and average return per view per day.
  • A green Target hit badge appears when a stock has reached the target you set.
  • Single chart: the price with every view marked. Marker size shows time spent that day, colour shows your label, and a star means you attached a rationale.
  • The NASDAQ line runs next to it, coloured as a heat map of how much attention you were paying at the time, from blue (low) to red (high).
  • Choose daily or hourly bars, a period from 1 week to since your first view, local currency or USD, and drag the range slider to zoom or pan. Fix a wrong symbol with the Yahoo symbol box.

2. Measure

Compare every position, or turn them into an index

Switch the chart between Single, Compare and Index to look at the same research from three angles.

  • Compare puts every tracked stock on one chart, each rebased to 100, so relative performance is easy to read.
  • Index combines your positions into a single portfolio equity curve, always with no leverage and no look-ahead: each day uses the previous day's weights.
  • Weighting: Equal, or Attention-weighted, where each name counts by its share of your total attention.
  • Holding rule: Attended = held and sold on Negative, Long only on Positive, or Hold everything attended.
2. Measure

Index statistics: the numbers behind your attention portfolio

Below the Index chart the dashboard measures the portfolio the way a fund would, on the window you are looking at, annualized with a risk-free rate of 0.

  • Allocation shows how concentrated your attention has made the portfolio.
  • Performance covers return, risk and drawdown.
  • Vs NASDAQ tells you whether the index beat simply holding the market, and why.
  • Weight vs contribution shows which stocks carried the result, with a holdings table.
Statistics measured

Allocation

Holdings
Names currently held under the selected holding rule.
Concentration (HHI)
Sum of squared weights. 1 means everything in one name; 1/N means spread equally over N names.
Effective holdings
1 / HHI: how many equal-weight names would give the same concentration.
Top holding
Weight of the largest position.
Top 3 weight
Combined weight of the three largest positions.
Avg turnover / bar
Average share of the portfolio reallocated per bar: 0.5 x the sum of absolute weight changes.

Performance

Total return
Index return over the displayed window.
Annualized return
Geometric return scaled to one year (needs at least 5 bars).
Volatility (ann.)
Standard deviation of bar returns, annualized.
Sharpe (rf=0)
Annualized mean bar return divided by annualized volatility.
Sortino
Like Sharpe, but only downside deviation in the denominator.
Max drawdown
Largest peak-to-trough decline of the index in the window.
Hit rate
Share of bars with a positive return.
Best bar / Worst bar
Best and worst single-bar return in the window.

Vs NASDAQ

NASDAQ return
NASDAQ buy-and-hold over the same window.
Active return
Index return minus NASDAQ return.
Beta
Sensitivity of index bar returns to NASDAQ bar returns.
Correlation
Pearson r of index versus NASDAQ bar returns.
Alpha (ann.)
Annualized mean return not explained by beta x NASDAQ.
Tracking error (ann.)
Annualized standard deviation of (index - NASDAQ) bar returns.
3. Understand

My Habits: how you actually research

Two columns, Views and Time, show your research from two angles, with Overview, Trends and Breakdown tabs and a Day, Week, Month or Year span.

  • Overview: usage summary, a year-long calendar heatmap and your market correlation.
  • Trends: views and active minutes over time, plus one line per stock to see what took your time and when.
  • Breakdown: by hour of day, weekday, sector, industry and most-attended stocks.
  • Market correlation compares your attention with NASDAQ returns on the previous day, the same day or the next day, next to the community's.

Longest gap in days with no views, including any current dry spell.

The single day with the most views (or most active time).

Market correlation

Correlation of your daily views (or minutes) with NASDAQ returns over a rolling window.

4. Calibrate

Predictions: every call scored against your own target and deadline

A call is a Positive or Negative label with a target price and horizon. Stocktrails follows each one to its deadline and keeps score.

  • Scorecard across all your calls, with four charts: pace to target, accuracy by horizon, track record over time and timing calibration.
  • A table of every prediction with entry price, target, current price, progress to target, time used, return, return versus NASDAQ and status (for example 52d left).
  • Filter the list by Open, Resolved or All.
Statistics measured

Scorecard

Open
Predictions whose deadline has not passed and whose target has not been reached.
Due within 7d
Open predictions whose deadline falls in the next seven days.
Resolved
Predictions that reached their target or ran past their deadline.
Direction hit rate
Share of resolved predictions where the price moved the way you called it.
Target hit rate
Share of resolved predictions with a usable target that actually reached it before the deadline.
Progress when missed
On expired predictions, how much of the required move was made. Negative means it went the other way.
Median time used
On targets reached, the median share of the horizon that had elapsed. Well under 50% means your horizons are longer than they need to be.
Brier score
Calibration of your direction calls against outcomes. Lower is better; 0.25 means no skill.
Edge vs NASDAQ
Average return of the call minus NASDAQ over the same window, signed by call direction. Above 0 means the calls beat holding the index.
4. Calibrate

Edge: do your labels actually carry information?

The Edge tab tests your outlook labels statistically, so you can tell skill from a good streak.

  • Event study: the average return after your Positive and after your Negative labels, at horizons from 1 day to 1 year.
  • Outlook mix over time: your Positive, Neutral and Negative labels per day, week or month.
  • Forecast accuracy over time, with a Daily, Weekly or Monthly view.
Statistics measured

Edge tab

After Positive / After Negative
Average return from the price when you labelled to N trading days later.
Signal edge
Return aligned with your call (Positive adds +return, Negative adds -return). Above 0 means your labels add value.
N
Sample size behind each row; a row needs labels old enough to have that much history.
Overall hit rate
Positive calls that rose plus Negative calls that fell, over non-neutral labelled events with 30 days of history.
Labelled events / Flip rate
How many labels you recorded, and the share that overwrote a different earlier label.
5. Compare

Community: see how your research stacks up

Three views put your habits next to everyone else's. Only aggregates are shared, and a ticker appears in Trending only once at least 3 users have viewed it.

  • Ranking over the last 30 days for views, research time, stocks covered and label hit rate, shown as top percent or as a rank in a small community.
  • You vs the crowd: a daily similarity score between your research mix and the community's, and with what the wider internet is watching.
  • Index: the community's most researched stocks built into an index, next to your own index and NASDAQ, with switchable lines for outside attention lists.
  • Trending now: which stocks are running hotter than usual today.
Statistics measured

Ranking and overlap

Views / Research time / Stocks covered
Your totals for the last 30 days, ranked against all active users.
Label hit rate
Your Positive and Negative calls that matched the move, ranked against other users.
Overlap
Daily cosine similarity between your ticker mix and the crowd's. 100% means you look at exactly what the crowd concentrates on; low means you research off the beaten path.
Outside lists
StockAnalysis pageviews, Reddit mentions, Stocktwits trending, Yahoo trending and Wikipedia pageviews, each compared with your mix on the days you researched.

Community index and trending

Weight / Users
A stock's share of the basket and how many users researched it.
First community view / Return since
When the community first looked at it and how it has done since.
Views today / 7d avg per day
Community views today against the previous seven-day average.
Expected today / Heat
Views expected at each stock's usual views per active user, and (views today + 1) / (expected + 1). A heat of 2x or more is flagged.

Scorecard

Pace: progress to target vs time used

One dot per open prediction. X = share of the horizon already spent, Y = share of the move to your target already made. The diagonal is on-pace: dots above it are ahead of schedule, dots below (and especially below 0) are behind. Green = Positive call, red = Negative.

Accuracy by horizon

Directional hit rate of resolved calls, grouped by the horizon you chose. The last bar is the 50% coin-flip baseline. Tells you which time frames you actually read well.

Track record over time

Cumulative directional hit rate as each prediction resolves, against the 50% baseline. Rising = you are getting better; the line is noisy until ~10 resolved calls.

Timing calibration

For targets that were reached: how much of the horizon had been used when they got there. Mass on the left = your targets arrive early (you could pick shorter horizons); mass on the right = you only just make it.

How you use Stocktrails

Section usage is synced to improve the app. No page content or personal data is included.

Data

Research from your browsing history

Add research Stocktrails did not see live: from before you installed it, or from another browser. Load a history file: Chrome via Google Takeout (deselect all → tick Chrome → export, unzip, pick History.json), or a CSV exported from another browser such as Comet (needs a URL column and a visit-time column; those exports list each page once with its last visit, so views are understated). Visits to the supported finance sites that are not already recorded are added; a stock already recorded at the same minute is skipped, so importing two overlapping files, or the same file twice, adds nothing twice. The file is read on this device; every other site in it is discarded and the file is not stored or uploaded. The added views sync to your account like live ones, without time on page, scroll depth or labels.
